A ball that lays its own track

The ball bounces up and to the right and drops a dashed trail as it goes. Nothing is drawn ahead of it: each dash appears only once the ball has passed over that spot. It settles mid-flight at the top right, which is exactly the static graphic you supplied.

The animation

Two bounces, a squash at each impact, then a slow hover once it lands. Hit replay to watch the trail appear.

Two ways to drop it in

Same graphic, different plumbing. Start with the SVG.

1 · Self-contained SVG — the safe one

snippets/bouncing-ball.svg. The animation is inside the SVG, so it needs no CSS and no JavaScript and survives a builder that strips style and script tags. Paste it inline, or upload it to your media library and place it as an ordinary image. It runs once on page load rather than on scroll — the one thing it gives up. To recolour, change the two #4f4f4f values.

2 · HTML block — runs on scroll

snippets/bouncing-ball.html, pasted whole into a Custom HTML or Code widget. It holds until the graphic is 35% in view, so a visitor sees the bounce happen rather than arriving after it finished.

How the trail works

Worth knowing before you retime anything.

The trail is 58 separate dashes, each one a short curve sitting on the flight path. Every dash starts invisible and appears at the moment the ball has travelled past its far end — so the trail is laid down behind the ball, not uncovered. Nothing masks or clips the arc.

That matters because the obvious approach is a mask: draw the whole dashed arc, then sweep a stroke along it to expose the dashes gradually. It looks identical when it works, and it fails badly when it doesn't — animating a mask's stroke-dashoffset on a <use> is dropped by some renderers, and sanitisers strip mask outright. Either way you get the whole arc at once with a ball running over the top of it. Individual dashes have nothing to drop: a renderer that can change opacity at all gets this right.

The dash positions and their reveal times are generated together with the ball's motion, from one set of easing curves — slow into each apex, quick into each bounce. Retiming the ball by hand puts the trail out of step, so change the values in the generator and re-run it instead: 

node snippets/tools/bounce-dashes.mjs

It measures the arc, fits a whole number of dashes to each flight so every segment starts and ends flush with one, works out when the ball passes each, and writes the result into the SVG, the HTML block, this page and the React component's data file.

Behaviour worth knowing

  • Under prefers-reduced-motion the finished graphic renders instantly — no bounce, no hover.
  • The graphic scales to its container's width; the ball and dashes scale with it. --bx-max-width stops it getting silly on a wide screen.
  • There is deliberate space below the arc: at each bounce the ball dips a radius below the trail, and without that room it would be clipped. Your static reference needed no such margin.
  • The svg origin is the ball's landing point, not the top left. That is what lets the ball sit in the right place before any animation has been applied — and it is why the coordinates are negative.
  • Each dash trails the ball by roughly a radius, so it surfaces from behind the ball rather than popping out of its centre. Nothing is ever visible ahead of the ball.
  • Nothing in either file mentions style or script tags in a comment any more. A sanitiser matching those tags with a regex will happily eat from a mention in a comment through to the real closing tag, taking the graphic with it.
